It <em>is</em> the AI.</p></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">Why It Matters</div><h3 class="reveal">You can fake a demo.<br>You cannot fake a foundation.</h3><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:1.8rem;">Anyone can call an AI API in ten lines. That skill expires the day the API changes.<br>The engineer who understands <em>why</em> it works can build, fix, and invent when the tools break.</p><p class="kicker reveal">Four subjects run the whole field: linear algebra, calculus, probability, information.</p></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">Foundation 1 · Linear Algebra</div><h3 class="reveal">Kolam Podalama?</h3><div class="tanglish-sub reveal">Pulligal sernthaa kolam, numbers sernthaa vector.</div><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:1.6rem;">Every word is just a dot in a Kolam. A vector is a list of numbers pointing to a specific dot. AI turns words, faces, and songs into these points. It measures meaning using <em>distance</em> between the dots.</p><p class="kicker reveal">The famous magic: King − Man + Woman ≈ Queen. Meaning becomes arithmetic.</p><img src="assets/kolam.png" class="person-img grn blend-screen" alt="Kolam Pattern" /></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">Foundation 1 · Linear Algebra</div><h3 class="reveal">Maavu Aatra Machine.</h3><div class="tanglish-sub reveal">Arisiya maava maathuradhu dhaan Matrix.</div><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:1.6rem;">If a vector is raw rice, a <b>matrix</b> is the grinder machine that stretches and reshapes it into batter. A neural network is just a series of these grinders stacked together.</p><p class="kicker reveal">"Deep" learning literally means: many transformations, stacked deep.</p></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">Foundation 2 · Calculus</div><h3 class="reveal">Erakkam Enga Irukku?</h3><div class="tanglish-sub reveal">Keezha pora vazhi edhu-nu kandupidikuradhu derivative.</div><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:1.6rem;">Forget the rules you memorised in school. In AI, a derivative simply tells the model, at any point, which direction reduces the error — and how steeply.</p></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">Foundation 2 · Calculus</div><h3 class="reveal">Kanna Kattikitu<br>Kodaikanal Erakkam.</h3><div class="tanglish-sub reveal">Kuruttu-thanama keezha irangurathu thaan Gradient Descent.</div><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:1.6rem;">Imagine standing on a foggy mountain, blindfolded, trying to reach the valley. You feel the slope with your feet and step downhill. Repeat a million times. That's how <em>every</em> AI model learns.</p><p class="kicker reveal">Step too big, you overshoot. Too small, you never arrive. That step size is the "learning rate."</p></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">Foundation 2 · The Chain Rule</div><h3 class="reveal">Kudumbathula<br>Yaar Mela Thappu?</h3><div class="tanglish-sub reveal">Thappu panna aala kandupidichu thiruthradhu thaan Backprop.</div><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:1.6rem;">When the network gets an answer wrong, backprop walks backwards through every layer asking "how much was <em>this</em> knob responsible for the mistake?" — then nudges each one. It's the chain rule, doing detective work.</p><p class="kicker reveal">This is Linnainmaa's 1970 idea. It trains every model you'll ever use.</p></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">Foundation 3 · Probability</div><h3 class="reveal">Pudhu Aadharam,<br>Pudhu Mudivu.</h3><div class="tanglish-sub reveal">Pudhu evidence kedacha pazhaya nambikkaiya maathikradhu.</div><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:1.6rem;">A test is "99% accurate" and you test positive for a rare disease. Are you doomed? Bayes' theorem says: probably not — because the disease is rare, most positives are false alarms.</p><p class="kicker reveal">Reverend Thomas Bayes worked this out in the 1740s. It now powers spam filters, medical AI, and self-driving cars.</p></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">Foundation 3 · Probability</div><h3 class="reveal">Nethu Ennachu-nu<br>Thevai Illa.</h3><div class="tanglish-sub reveal">Nalai-kku nadakkurathu innaiku nadakradha vechu thaan.</div><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:1.6rem;">Tomorrow's weather depends on today's — not on last month's. <b>Andrey Markov</b> turned that simple idea into chains that model language, finance, and Google's original PageRank.</p><p class="kicker reveal">Your phone's "next word" suggestion is a great-grandchild of Markov's 1900s maths.</p></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">Foundation 4 · Statistics</div><h3 class="reveal">The bell curve is the<br>shape of almost everything.</h3><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:1.6rem;">Heights, exam scores, measurement errors — pile up into the same bell shape. <b>Carl Friedrich Gauss</b> mapped it 200 years ago. AI assumes it constantly: to model noise, uncertainty, and "normal" behaviour.</p></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">Foundation 4 · Information Theory</div><h3 class="reveal">Chennai-la Snow Peidha?</h3><div class="tanglish-sub reveal">Ethirpaarkadha vishayam thaan Information!</div><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:1.6rem;">"The sun rose today" tells you nothing — zero surprise, zero information. "It snowed in Chennai" is huge news. <b>Claude Shannon</b> made surprise measurable, and called it <em>entropy</em>.</p><p class="kicker reveal">Every time an AI model trains, it is literally minimising surprise. That number is its loss.</p></section><section class="slide"><div class="eyebrow grn reveal">The Whole Field, In One Line</div><h2 class="reveal grad-grn">Roll downhill<br>to reduce surprise.</h2><p class="lead reveal" style="margin-top:2rem;">Vectors hold the meaning. Calculus finds the way down. Probability handles the doubt.
